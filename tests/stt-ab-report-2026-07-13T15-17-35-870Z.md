## Accuracy by category (WER — lower is better)

WER counts every substituted, inserted and deleted WORD against the reference. CER does
the same over characters, which matters in Hebrew: prefixes glue onto words (בבית = "in
the house"), so an engine that writes "ב בית" is one space from correct but WER scores it
as a total loss. Trust CER when the two disagree on a near-miss.

### What this test cannot tell you

The corpus is **Cartesia-synthesized speech, not human speech**. TTS audio is unnaturally
well-articulated: no disfluencies, no mumbling, no accent, no two words running together.
Real callers do all of that constantly.

So the WER here is a **ceiling** — real Hebrew WER will be worse for BOTH engines. This is
a **comparative** instrument. "Soniox beat OpenAI by 3x on our corpus" is supportable;
"Soniox achieves 1.2% WER on our calls" is not.

We have made exactly this mistake before: the synthetic caller harness measured end-of-turn
at 258ms against audio containing digital silence, while a real phone measured ~950ms with
identical config (docs/phase-4-known-issues.md §5). The `.noisy` condition exists to fight
that, but it is still simulated. **Shadow mode on real callers is what settles this.**
