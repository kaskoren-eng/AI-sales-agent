## P0-1 — she asked his name three times, then let a garbled turn rename him

Two failures, one cause: nothing held the difference between a fact we HAVE and a fact we WANT.

**New: `fact-memory.ts`.** Per call, shared between the agent and the tool runtime.
- Counts the questions in her COMMITTED utterances (not the model's intent — what the lead reacted
  to was what he heard). Two of her three name-asks on that call were improvised phrasings absent
  from the prompt's own bank, so the detector reads the transcript as well as the bank.
- Appends a turn-boundary note: what is established, and what she has asked for twice already and
  must stop asking. Same injection point and cache discipline as the phrase ledger.
- Guards identity writes in `capture_lead_info`.

**The decision the commit message was asked to carry: may `capture_lead_info` replace a non-null
field mid-call?** For `name` / `phone` / `email`: **no**, not without the new `is_correction`
argument. For everything else — business, pain point, budget, timeline, qualification, notes —
**yes, unchanged**; "call it again whenever a fact changes" is the right rule there.

The asymmetry is about what is AUDIBLE, not about write hygiene. An identity is spoken aloud
constantly from the moment it is learned, so a wrong one reaches the caller within seconds and
cannot be withdrawn; a wrong budget is silent bookkeeping the next tool call fixes. And the DB had
already refused this rename — `upsertLead` coalesces and cannot blank a name — so the tool accepting
it produced the worst of both worlds: the CRM kept `קורן` while she said `טל`. The refusal now
travels back in the **tool result**, which the next inference step reads before she can speak.

Enrichment is explicitly not a rename: `קורן` → `קורן שטרית` is him giving his surname, which Step 4
asks her to collect. Token containment in either direction is allowed.

**Prompt half:** a "Call Memory" section — a given fact is settled, an unanswered question gets at
most one repeat, and a stray noun in a later turn is a mishearing rather than a correction. Prompt
and code are behind the same switch so they can never describe different rules.

## P0-2 — the value proposition inverted

`ועוזרים לא לפספס לידים` → the lead heard `מה עוזרים לו לפספס?`. The value proposition is not a fixed
string anywhere in the prompt — the model composes it from Role + Business Context — so there was
nothing to patch and the fix is a rule: never let meaning rest on one small unstressed word, prefer
the positive statement of the same fact, and when a negative is meant, mark it twice.

**The sweep** found five more fixed lines that invert into a plausible, harmful sentence rather than
into noise. All five now say the positive version; the worst was the opt-out promise
(`לא נתקשר אליך יותר` heard without its particle is a promise to keep calling someone who asked us to
stop → `אני מסירה אותך מרשימת הפניות שלנו`). A test now sweeps every verbatim line so a future edit
cannot reintroduce one.

Lines beginning `אין` were deliberately left alone: it is a full stressed word and dropping it yields
ungrammatical noise, not an opposite.

## P1-1 — the repetition metric was green through the repetition

`repeatedPhraseCount: 0` was correct and useless: a 4-gram starting at a one-word opener runs into
the sentence behind it, and that sentence differs every time. `countRepeatedOpeners` now counts
distinct repeated openers and is folded into `repeatedPhraseCount`; `repeatedOpenerCount` keeps the
halves legible so the 4-gram figure still compares with the humanization baseline. On that call it
would have read 3.

The no-repeat rule was never going to fix the sound — over eight turns, three words cannot be spread
more thinly than "each one twice". So the bank widened to five and is spent like a shuffled deck.

## P1-3 — the register, and a correction to the plan

**`וואלה` is not an invented word.** It passed round 4b — the same listening screen that banned
written laughter — and `EMOTIONAL_COLOR` quotes it as the surprise device. She reached for an
approved word from the section next door. The real defect was that two screened banks lived in two
sections with no name covering both, so nothing could say what "the register vocabulary" was.
`REGISTER_VOCABULARY` is now their union (5 slang + 3 devices = 8), read by the prompt, the tracker
and the metric alike.

**Bound or open — decided: bounded.** The case for invention is real (variation is the point, eight
words is not many) and it loses to one fact: an unscreened Hebrew interjection fails SILENTLY. `חח`
arrives as the spelled letter khet, `אוו` was swallowed whole, and nothing in the transcript or the
metrics shows it — the first person to find out is a lead. Widening stays cheap: run the screening,
then add.

**Hit rate:** the quota now names a floor and a countable trigger, and `register-tracker.ts` notices
two dry replies in a row and says so at the turn boundary — the same guidance/enforcement split as
the phrase ledger, which this exact section already needed once. `registerTouchPct` in the call
report makes the next call answerable instead of arguable (biased to over-count, so a low reading is
a real miss).

## P1-2 — the measurement, which is the main finding

**The 5.7s and 6.2s are not silence.** They are the interval between two transcript timestamps, and
`recordTranscript` stamps `Date.now()` inside the `ConversationItemAdded` handler. The SDK emits
that event from `_conversationItemAdded`, which runs *after* `agentStoppedSpeakingAt` and after the
playout tasks complete (`agent_activity.js`, the pipeline reply path) — **an assistant message
commits when its audio has finished playing.** So the interval between two commits is
`silence + the whole of the second reply's speaking time`, and a four-second Hebrew sentence
accounts for most of a six-second "gap".

Arithmetic on the two cases in the plan, using the tool-completion stamps in the same report
(`recordToolCall` stamps at completion, so tool start = `atMs − durationMs`):

| receipt commits | tool completes | reply commits | commit interval | tool | left for TTFT+TTS+**speaking** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90896 | 90946 (979ms) | 97082 | 6186ms | 979 | 5207ms |
| 103441 | 103904 (1074ms) | 109172 | 5731ms | 1074 | 4794ms |

Subtract a second inference step's TTFT (~1021ms on this call) and TTS first byte (~236ms) and about
**4s and 3.5s remain** — which is the length of the replies themselves, not a hole.

**The silence was never measured at all**, and that is the real instrumentation gap. `deadAir` closes
its stopwatch on her *first* audio of a turn (`noteAgentStartedSpeaking` clears `#userStoppedAt`), so
on a tool-calling turn it measures the wait before the receipt — 1536ms median, healthy — and then
stops looking. Everything after the receipt, where the tool round-trip and the second step live, had
no instrument. That is why the plan's number and the dead-air median could both be true and
contradictory.

**What I added (measurement only):** the SDK already puts `startedSpeakingAt` / `stoppedSpeakingAt`
on every committed message and we were discarding both.
- `TranscriptLine.spokeAtMs` / `spokeUntilMs` — when her voice actually started and stopped.
  `atMs` deliberately keeps its commit-clock meaning so every earlier report still compares.
- `summary.agentGap` — median / max / samples plus every gap, each with the tools that ran inside it
  and `toolMs`. A gap is only counted between two assistant lines with no caller turn between.
- A live log line per gap: `latency agent_gap ms=… toolMs=… unexplainedMs=… tools=…`.

**Predicted, to be confirmed on the next call:** post-receipt silence ≈ tool (~1s) + next-step TTFT
(~1s) + TTS TTFB (~0.24s) ≈ **2.2s** — the previous session's estimate, roughly half what the plan
measured. **Blocking behaviour is unchanged, deliberately.** Making `capture_lead_info` non-blocking
moves when `rt.leadId` resolves for a later `book_meeting` and would hide write failures; it needs
its own switch and tests.

⚠️ If the measured remainder comes back much larger than ~2.2s after subtracting `toolMs`, the
balance is end-of-turn detection. `docs/phase-4-known-issues.md` §4 puts that at ~700ms and there is
no Hebrew turn detector (which is why `@livekit/agents-plugin-livekit` was dropped on main). That is
a finding to report, not to act on.

2. **The two new acknowledgements** (`הבנתי אותך.` and `טוב, הבנתי.`). Neither has been heard on a
   phone line — there is no round-5 screening for acknowledgements as there is for the slang bank —

   is thinking in. If either sounds wrong, `VOICE_ACK_LEDGER_ENABLED=false` restores the three-word

3. **P0-1 end to end.** Give the name once and check she never asks again; then say something noisy
   that could be a name and check she keeps calling you by the first one. Grep the log for

   go through, after she repeats it back.
4. **The register hit rate.** `registerTouchPct` in the report, and the ear test that matters: does
   Koren notice any everyday words at all? Grep `coach_note` for `registerNudge`.
5. **The P1-2 numbers.** `latency agent_gap` per gap, and `summary.agentGap` in the report. This is
   the measurement the whole item exists for.
6. **Latency as usual** — EOU / model TTFT / TTS first byte / worst case / dead air. Nothing here
   adds a stage to the speech path; the coach note rides the existing turn-boundary injection and the
   ack still leaves first and unheld.

## What I decided against, and why

- **A speech-guard rewrite of `לא` + infinitive into positive Hebrew.** A regex cannot conjugate
  Hebrew, and the guard's own standing rule is that phrasing is an authoring problem fixed at
  generation time.
- **Niqqud on `לא` to give it stress.** Plausible, and unverifiable without an ear on a real call.
  Blanket niqqud is already a recorded dead end. Worth trying if the prompt rule alone does not do it.
- **Changing the FAQ answer `לא, אנחנו בונים סוכנים … - לא תסריט קבוע.`** Its second half can invert
  ("a fixed script") but its first half already carries the answer, so a dropped particle degrades it
  rather than reversing it — and it is persona data, pinned byte-for-byte because it is Koren's own
  tuned Hebrew. **Flagged for his ear; changing a persona string is his call.**
- **Widening the acknowledgement bank further.** Every candidate has to survive being read back after
  a QUESTION (the rule that removed `כן.`) and must not be a word she opens replies with herself.
  That eliminates almost every short Hebrew receipt: `טוב.` is heard as "I'm good", `ברור.` and
  `נכון.` as agreement, `הבנתי.` and `מעולה.` are hers. Two compound receipts were the honest limit.
- **Telling the model about the acknowledgement deck.** She does not write those words, we do. A note
  asking her to vary a word she never chose would be a lie about her own turn.
- **Reporting `repeatedPhraseCount` as 4-grams only.** The plan asked for a counter that reflects
  reality; a metric that stays green through its own defect is worse than none. The 4-gram figure is
  still separately readable as the baseline currency.
- **Making `capture_lead_info` non-blocking.** In scope for measurement only. See P1-2.
- **Any P2 character work.** Out of scope by instruction, and correctly sequenced after these.
